About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in transportation engineering at the University of Minnesota, working in the Transit Lab with Dr. Alireza Khani, and I hold a master's in data science and operations research from Industrial and Systems Engineering. My first degree was in electrical engineering, where I worked on neural control for autonomous vehicles.

My research focuses on logistics and fleet decisions at scale. I turn messy, real-world planning questions into optimization models that decide where to place charging infrastructure, how large a fleet to buy, and how to stage a multi-year transition within a fixed budget. I build and test these models on real agency data, not synthetic instances, so the answers hold up under real operating constraints. On the freight side, I work on a MnDOT truck project that turns large truck flow and highway corridor datasets into clear statewide pictures of how freight moves, so agencies can plan with it.

The part I care most about is what happens after the model works. Two of my projects shipped as open source tools that Minnesota public agencies use directly, and the underlying study became a state technical report and a public webinar. I am looking for research and applied data science roles where modeling, data engineering, and shipping the result are the same job.

Research

Optimization under budget constraints

Multi-stage charger deployment and fleet sizing for heterogeneous fleets, solved on real agency data.

Mechanism design and incentives

Incentive compatible schemes for crowdsourced delivery and bike rebalancing, where participation is a choice.

Freight and passenger integration

Using transit networks and crowd-shipped bikes for last-mile delivery without degrading passenger service.

Network modeling at scale

Transportation network analysis, truck flow estimation, and corridor level visualization across statewide networks.

Learning and control

Reinforcement learning and dynamic programming for sequential decisions, plus earlier work on neural control of autonomous vehicles.

Research to production

Turning models into documented open source tools that agency staff run without a modeler present.
